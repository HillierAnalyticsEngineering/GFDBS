How our team designs a CubeSat today.

A mission lead writes a one-page brief: the payload, the orbit, the life, and the launch mass limit.

A power engineer sizes the solar array against the orbit-average load, and checks that it still closes at end of life.
A communications engineer closes the downlink to our ground station at the lowest elevation we use.
A thermal engineer screens the hot and cold cases to keep the bulk temperature inside limits.
A radiation engineer checks that the parts survive the total dose for the mission life.
A mass properties engineer tracks mass with growth allowance against the launch limit.

They work mostly in parallel and trade numbers by email. The transmitter is a load on the power system.
The electrical load becomes heat. Bigger panels and more shielding cost mass. The lead systems engineer
holds a review, finds what does not add up across the disciplines, and sends people back to rework.
